Program analytics · 2026 cycle

VOTE-E Dashboard

Reporting for a chatbot that helps young people register and plan to vote.

ResultShowed YouTube content converting best, which moved partner outreach from Twitch alone to Twitch and YouTube.

BigQueryFunnel analysisAttributionVoter file match
The question
The program team needed one place to see who the chatbot reached, where people dropped off, and which partners and sources brought in people who went on to register.
The data
Conversation logs, registration link clicks and completions, vote plan steps, partner and source attribution, and voter file matches for people who shared a name.
What I built
An 11-tab dashboard: impact toplines, a question-to-registration funnel, a vote plan funnel, partner and source breakdowns, a voter file profile of users, question topics by week, and a state map with day-by-day playback.
Outcome
The program team checked it throughout the 2026 cycle, and it refreshed daily from BigQuery. When the vote plan flow launched in October, I added it as a second funnel so the team could see exactly where people stopped. We moved partner outreach from Twitch alone to Twitch and YouTube after the source breakdown showed YouTube content converting best.

Method notes

  • Every funnel step uses the same denominator, so drop-offs compare directly.
  • Conversation times use the voter's local time from their state on file, fall back to Eastern, and show what share had a state.
  • Demographics include only voter-file-matched people, with the match rate shown beside them.

Election Night Tracker

Campus turnout and vote share against comparison campuses, plus every race leadership was watching.

ResultLeadership and the Communications, Social and Development teams used it live to report campus results against a fair comparison.

AP resultsPrecinct geographyLive refreshComparison design
The question
Did turnout and Democratic vote share at our campuses surpass past years, and did our core campuses outperform comparison campuses? How were the Senate, House, governor and state legislative races going?
The data
Precinct-level campus results pulled from state and county election sites on election night, and AP results in BigQuery refreshed through the night.
What I built
Campus toplines, core vs. comparison charts across three cycles, a precincts-reported tracker, and chamber control views for federal and state legislative races.

Method notes

  • Comparing core campuses with comparison campuses separates program effects from statewide shifts.
  • Precinct IDs that didn't match the official 2026 boundaries were fixed with a manual crosswalk for split and merged precincts.
  • North Carolina uses Election Day votes only in every cycle, so years compare like for like.
Trend analysis · 2018 / 2022 / 2026

Youth Early Vote Comparisons

Is youth early voting ahead of past cycles, while voting is still under way?

ResultFlagged low youth ballot return rates in Michigan and Pennsylvania, which led to an extra text and social campaign in both states.

Time-aligned comparisonBigQueryState drill-down
The question
Leadership wanted to know each morning whether 18–29 early voting was ahead of 2022 and 2018, nationally and in priority states.
The data
Daily early vote and ballot request files by age and party for three cycles.
What I built
National, priority-state and all-state views: cumulative curves by days before Election Day, youth share of all early voters, party mix, and ballot return rates.
Outcome
It gave leadership a same-day read on youth early voting in every state until early voting ended, always compared with 2022 and 2018 at the same point. Because each cycle used the same structure, adding 2026 meant loading new files rather than rebuilding. When we saw low youth ballot return rates in Michigan and Pennsylvania, we ran an extra text and social campaign encouraging young voters in those states to return their ballots.

Method notes

  • Each cycle is cut at the same number of days before Election Day, so 2026 is never compared with a finished 2022.
  • Return rates leave out all-mail states, where every voter gets a ballot.
  • National totals count only states with data in both cycles being compared.
  • 2018 has no request dates, so it shows no return rate instead of a misleading one.
Program evaluation · Contact data

Contact Method Analysis

How texted voters compared with young voters we didn't reach.

ResultTexted voters had lower predicted turnout than voters we didn't reach, yet voted early at a similar rate.

BigQueryComparison groupsTurnout scores
The question
Did people we texted request and return ballots at higher rates than similar people we never contacted?
The data
Text and call history, the voter status list of ballot requests and early votes, and modeled turnout scores.
What I built
A BigQuery pipeline comparing each contact method with an 18–29 no-contact group by state, reporting ballot return rate, early vote rate and turnout score distributions.
What it showed
Texted voters had lower predicted turnout than the no-contact group but voted early at a similar rate. Without a randomized control group this is descriptive, and the write-up said so.

Method notes

  • Counts are unique people per method, so repeat texts don't inflate the totals.
  • The no-contact group is limited to the same ages and states as the program.
  • Temp tables read the national voter file once, which kept query costs down.

Post-election review · 2024 cycle

2024 Program Review

Who our registration, pledge and call programs reached, and how the people we contacted turned out.

ResultBecame the starting point for the 2026 program and its shift toward online and multi-channel outreach.

Voter file matchTurnout analysisMulti-channel outreachLooker Studio
The question
After the 2024 election, leadership wanted to know how many people our programs reached, who they were, and whether people we contacted more often or through more channels turned out at higher rates.
The data
Voter registration and pledge-to-vote records matched to the voter file, call attempt and conversation logs, text and mail program lists, 2024 vote history, and youth turnout from 2016 to 2024 for context.
What I built
A multi-page review: totals collected, match rates and turnout by program; call reach by demographic; turnout by number of conversations and by combination of contact types; and youth turnout trends by party, race and gender.
What it showed
Turnout rose with each added type of contact, from registration alone up to registration, text, call and mail, even though those voters had lower predicted turnout going in. Without a randomized control this is descriptive, but it is the pattern a multi-channel program hopes to see.
Outcome
The review became the starting point for our 2026 program. Based on what it showed, we shifted toward more online and multi-channel outreach. It also gave us the numbers to show partners and funders the reach and results of our work.

Method notes

  • Turnout appears next to modeled turnout scores, so groups that were already likely to vote don't look like program wins.
  • Pledges and registrations are reported separately, each with its own voter file match rate.
  • Youth turnout is shown beside total turnout for each year, so presidential and midterm swings aren't mistaken for youth trends.

SQL samples

BigQuery patterns from the projects above, with table names made generic. Each one solves a problem that would otherwise give wrong numbers or a large bill.

Collapse before you join

The voter status list can have several rows per person.

Joining it directly would duplicate contacts and inflate every count. Collapsing it first with LOGICAL_OR keeps one row per voter and marks them true if any row is true.

WITH status AS (
  SELECT
    voter_id,
    LOGICAL_OR(voted_flag)                          AS early_voted,
    LOGICAL_OR(ballot_requested_flag OR voted_flag) AS requested_ballot
  FROM `project.voterfile.voter_status`
  GROUP BY voter_id
),

-- one row per person per method, so repeat contacts aren't double counted
contacted AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT method, voter_id
  FROM `project.contacts.texts_and_calls`
)

SELECT
  c.method,
  COUNT(c.voter_id)                                        AS targets,
  COUNTIF(s.requested_ballot)                               AS ballot_requests,
  COUNTIF(s.early_voted)                                    AS early_votes,
  SAFE_DIVIDE(COUNTIF(s.early_voted), COUNTIF(s.requested_ballot)) AS return_rate,
  SAFE_DIVIDE(COUNTIF(s.early_voted), COUNT(c.voter_id))         AS early_vote_rate
FROM contacted c
LEFT JOIN status s ON c.voter_id = s.voter_id
GROUP BY c.method;

A comparison group that survives NULLs

The no-contact group is every 18–29 voter in the program states who never got a text or call.

NOT IN returns nothing at all if the contact list holds a single NULL ID. NOT EXISTS gives the right answer either way.

no_contact AS (
  SELECT
    'no contact' AS method,
    v.voter_id
  FROM `project.voterfile.national_file` v
  WHERE v.age BETWEEN 18 AND 29
    AND v.state IN ('GA', 'MI', 'NC')   -- same states as the program
    AND NOT EXISTS (
      SELECT 1
      FROM contacted c
      WHERE c.voter_id = v.voter_id
    )
),

all_groups AS (
  SELECT method, voter_id FROM contacted
  UNION ALL
  SELECT method, voter_id FROM no_contact
)

Read the big table once

BigQuery bills by data read, and a CTE can be re-run every time a query references it.

Saving a narrow, filtered copy of the national voter file as a temp table means the expensive read happens once. Every later step reads the small copy.

-- 1. contacts: read once
CREATE TEMP TABLE contacted AS
SELECT DISTINCT method, voter_id
FROM `project.contacts.texts_and_calls`;

-- 2. national file: only the rows and columns needed
CREATE TEMP TABLE voters_slim AS
SELECT voter_id, age, state, turnout_score
FROM `project.voterfile.national_file`
WHERE (age BETWEEN 18 AND 29 AND state IN ('GA', 'MI', 'NC'))
   OR voter_id IN (SELECT voter_id FROM contacted);

-- 3. every later step joins voters_slim, not the national file
SELECT
  v.state,
  g.method,
  ROUND(AVG(v.turnout_score) / 100, 4)                        AS avg_turnout_score,
  APPROX_QUANTILES(v.turnout_score, 100)[OFFSET(50)] / 100 AS median_turnout_score
FROM all_groups g
LEFT JOIN voters_slim v ON g.voter_id = v.voter_id
GROUP BY v.state, g.method;

Fixing precincts that don't line up

Results and official boundaries agreed on almost every precinct ID. About 25 statewide didn't, mostly precincts that were split or merged.

A small hand-checked crosswalk maps each odd shape ID to its results ID. Pieces of a split precinct merge back into one polygon, so vote counts land on the right shape.

precinct_crosswalk AS (
  SELECT * FROM UNNEST([
    STRUCT('LENOIR' AS county, 'PH1' AS shapes_pid, 'PH' AS results_pid),
    STRUCT('LENOIR',           'PH2',               'PH')
  ])
),

precinct_shapes AS (
  SELECT
    UPPER(TRIM(s.county_name))                          AS county_name,
    COALESCE(x.results_pid, UPPER(TRIM(s.precinct_id))) AS precinct_id,
    ST_UNION_AGG(s.geometry)                            AS geometry
  FROM `project.geo.precinct_shapes` s
  LEFT JOIN precinct_crosswalk x
    ON  UPPER(TRIM(s.county_name)) = x.county
    AND UPPER(TRIM(s.precinct_id)) = x.shapes_pid
  WHERE TRIM(s.precinct_id) != ''
  GROUP BY 1, 2
)
